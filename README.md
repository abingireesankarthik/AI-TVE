# Section 1: AI in Your Professional Work — Awareness and Reality Check

<span class="time-chip">⏱ 8–10 minutes</span>

--{{0}}--
Let's begin by grounding ourselves in what AI actually looks like in everyday professional work.

## What Is AI in the Workplace?

--{{1}}--
Artificial intelligence in the workplace rarely looks like science fiction. It looks like a chatbot drafting an email, a search tool summarising research, or a spreadsheet assistant spotting a pattern in your data.

Workplace AI typically refers to tools that can generate text, summarise information, answer questions, or support decision-making — without replacing human judgement.

### Where Employees Already Use AI

- ✍️ Drafting emails and reports (ChatGPT, Copilot)
- 🔎 Researching policies and evidence (Consensus, Perplexity)
- 📊 Analysing data and spreadsheets (Copilot in Excel)
- 🗓️ Summarising meetings and notes

### Risks of Uncritical AI Use

<div class="warning-box">

⚠️ **Why caution matters**

AI tools can produce confident-sounding but **incorrect** information (known as "hallucination"), can reflect **biases** present in their training data, and may **expose confidential information** if sensitive data is entered into them.

</div>

--{{0}}--
AI competency is not about becoming a programmer. It's about using these tools responsibly, critically, and ethically.

### Why AI Competency Matters

This module does not require coding or technical expertise. It requires **critical thinking, ethical awareness, and responsible practice** — the same professional judgement you already apply in your HR role.

# Section 2: What Competencies Do Professionals Need?

<span class="time-chip">⏱ 12–15 minutes</span>

--{{0}}--
Now we turn to the five competency areas adapted from the UNESCO AI Competency Framework that every professional should develop.

## 1. Human-Centred AI

--{{1}}--
Human-centred AI means that artificial intelligence supports human decisions rather than replacing professional judgement. In HR specifically, this means AI can assist with drafting or research, but final decisions about people must always remain with a qualified human.

<div class="lo-box">

**Key principle:** AI is a *decision-support* tool, not a *decision-maker*. Recruitment, disciplinary, and performance decisions must retain meaningful human oversight.

</div>

## 2. AI Ethics

--{{0}}--
Ethical awareness sits at the heart of responsible AI use, and it rests on four pillars.

| Principle | What It Means in Practice |
|---|---|
| **Bias** | AI can replicate and amplify biases from its training data — especially risky in recruitment or performance screening |
| **Fairness** | Outcomes should be equitable across all groups of employees and candidates |
| **Transparency** | Employees should know when and how AI is being used in decisions that affect them |
| **Privacy** | Personal and confidential data must be protected from exposure to AI systems |

![Diagram showing four pillars of AI ethics: bias, fairness, transparency, and privacy, arranged around a central balance scale icon](https://cdn-icons-png.flaticon.com/512/2103/2103832.png "Four pillars of responsible AI ethics")

## 3. AI Foundations

--{{1}}--
A basic understanding of how generative AI works will help you use it more critically.

- **What AI is:** systems trained on large datasets to recognise patterns and generate predictions or text
- **Generative AI:** a type of AI (like ChatGPT) that creates new text, images, or content based on prompts
- **Limitations:** AI can "hallucinate" — producing fluent, confident, but factually incorrect information

<div class="warning-box">
⚠️ **Hallucination example:** An AI tool might confidently cite a labour law, policy, or statistic that does not actually exist. Always verify factual claims against a trusted source before using them in professional work.
</div>

## 4. AI Tool Selection

--{{0}}--
Different AI tools are built for different purposes. Choosing the right tool is itself a professional competency.
